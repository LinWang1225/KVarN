

To solve the problem, we are given that the polynomial  
$$
f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x - 1
$$  
is a factor of  
$$
g(x) = x^9 + px^6 + qx^3 + r
$$  
and we are to find the ordered triple $(p, q, r)$.

---

### Step 1: Use the fact that $ f(x) \mid g(x) $

If $ f(x) $ divides $ g(x) $, then every root $ \alpha $ of $ f(x) $ must also be a root of $ g(x) $. That is, for any root $ \alpha $ of $ f(x) $, we must have:
$$
g(\alpha) = \alpha^9 + p\alpha^6 + q\alpha^3 + r = 0
$$

This gives us a key equation:
$$
\alpha^9 = -p\alpha^6 - q\alpha^3 - r
$$

---

### Step 2: Express higher powers of $ \alpha $ in terms of lower powers

Since $ \alpha $ is a root of $ f(x) $, we know:
$$
\alpha^3 = 3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1
$$

We can use this to express higher powers of $ \alpha $ in terms of $ \alpha^2, \alpha, $ and constants. Let's compute up to $ \alpha^9 $:

- $ \alpha^3 = 3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1 $
- $ \alpha^4 = \alpha \cdot \alpha^3 = \alpha(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) = 3\alpha^3 - 4\alpha^2 + \alpha = 3(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) - 4\alpha^2 + \alpha = 5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 3 $
- $ \alpha^5 = \alpha \cdot \alpha^4 = \alpha(5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 3) = 5\alpha^3 - 11\alpha^2 + 3\alpha = 5(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) - 11\alpha^2 + 3\alpha = 4\alpha^2 - 17\alpha + 5 $
- $ \alpha^6 = \alpha \cdot \alpha^5 = \alpha(4\alpha^2 - 17\alpha + 5) = 4\alpha^3 - 17\alpha^2 + 5\alpha = 4(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) - 17\alpha^2 + 5\alpha = -5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 4 $
- $ \alpha^7 = \alpha \cdot \alpha^6 = \alpha(-5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 4) = -5\alpha^3 - 11\alpha^2 + 4\alpha = -5(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) - 11\alpha^2 + 4\alpha = -26\alpha^2 + 24\alpha - 5 $
- $ \alpha^8 = \alpha \cdot \alpha^7 = \alpha(-26\alpha^2 + 24\alpha - 5) = -26\alpha^3 + 24\alpha^2 - 5\alpha = -26(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) + 24\alpha^2 - 5\alpha = -54\alpha^2 + 99\alpha - 26 $
- $ \alpha^9 = \alpha \cdot \alpha^8 = \alpha(-54\alpha^2 + 99\alpha - 26) = -54\alpha^3 + 99\alpha^2 - 26\alpha = -54(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) + 99\alpha^2 - 26\alpha = -63\alpha^2 + 190\alpha - 54 $

---

### Step 3: Plug into the equation $ \alpha^9 + p\alpha^6 + q\alpha^3 + r = 0 $

Substitute the expressions for $ \alpha^9, \alpha^6, \alpha^3 $ into the equation:
$$
(-63\alpha^2 + 190\alpha - 54) + p(-5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 4) + q(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) + r = 0
$$

Group like terms:
- $ \alpha^2 $: $ -63 - 5p + 3q $
- $ \alpha $: $ 190 - 11p - 4q $
- Constant: $ -54 + 4p + q + r $

Set each coefficient to zero:
1. $ -63 - 5p + 3q = 0 $
2. $ 190 - 11p - 4q = 0 $
3. $ -54 + 4p + q + r = 0 $

---

### Step 4: Solve the system of equations

From equation (1):  
$$
-5p + 3q = 63 \quad \text{(1)}
$$

From equation (2):  
$$
-11p - 4q = -190 \quad \text{(2)}
$$

Multiply (1) by 11 and (2) by 5 to eliminate $ p $:

- (1) × 11: $ -55p + 33q = 693 $
- (2) × 5: $ -55p - 20q = -950 $

Subtract the second from the first:
$$
(33q + 20q) = 693 + 950 \Rightarrow 53q = 1643 \Rightarrow q = 31
$$

Substitute $ q = 31 $ into (1):
$$
-5p + 3(31) = 63 \Rightarrow -5p + 93 = 63 \Rightarrow -5p = -30 \Rightarrow p = 6
$$

Now use equation (3) to find $ r $:
$$
-54 + 4(6) + 31 + r = 0 \Rightarrow -54 + 24 + 31 + r = 0 \Rightarrow -1 + r = 0 \Rightarrow r = -1
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{(6, 31, -1)}
$$
