

To simplify the expression  
$$
\frac{4 + 6a}{5} - \frac{1 + 3a}{4}
$$  
into a single fraction, we follow these steps:

---

### **Step 1: Find the Least Common Denominator (LCD)**

The denominators are 5 and 4. The least common denominator is the least common multiple (LCM) of 5 and 4, which is **20**.

---

### **Step 2: Rewrite Each Fraction with the LCD**

We rewrite each fraction so that the denominator is 20:

- For $\frac{4 + 6a}{5}$, multiply numerator and denominator by 4:
  $$
  \frac{4(4 + 6a)}{20} = \frac{16 + 24a}{20}
  $$

- For $\frac{1 + 3a}{4}$, multiply numerator and denominator by 5:
  $$
  \frac{5(1 + 3a)}{20} = \frac{5 + 15a}{20}
  $$

---

### **Step 3: Subtract the Two Fractions**

Now that both fractions have the same denominator, we subtract the numerators:
$$
\frac{16 + 24a}{20} - \frac{5 + 15a}{20} = \frac{(16 + 24a) - (5 + 15a)}{20}
$$

Simplify the numerator:
$$
16 - 5 = 11,\quad 24a - 15a = 9a
$$

So the combined numerator is:
$$
11 + 9a
$$

---

### **Step 4: Write the Final Answer**

The expression as a single fraction is:
$$
\frac{11 + 9a}{20}
$$

Alternatively, it can be written as:
$$
\frac{9a + 11}{20}
$$

Both forms are algebraically equivalent, but typically, the standard form is to write the variable term first, so:

$$
\boxed{\dfrac{9a + 11}{20}}
$$
