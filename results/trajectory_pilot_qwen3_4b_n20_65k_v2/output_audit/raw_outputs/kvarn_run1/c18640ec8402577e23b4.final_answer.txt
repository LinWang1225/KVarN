

To determine the **range** of the function  
$$
y = \log_2 (\sqrt{\sin x})
$$  
for $ 0^\circ < x < 180^\circ $, we begin by analyzing the behavior of the function within the given domain.

---

### Step 1: Simplify the Function

We can simplify the expression using logarithmic identities:

$$
\log_2 (\sqrt{\sin x}) = \log_2 (\sin x)^{1/2} = \frac{1}{2} \log_2 (\sin x)
$$

So the function becomes:
$$
y = \frac{1}{2} \log_2 (\sin x)
$$

---

### Step 2: Determine the Range of $\sin x$ in the Given Interval

The sine function, $\sin x$, is **positive** for all $ x \in (0^\circ, 180^\circ) $, and it reaches its **maximum value of 1** at $ x = 90^\circ $, and approaches **0** as $ x \to 0^\circ $ or $ x \to 180^\circ $.

Thus, the range of $\sin x$ in this interval is:
$$
(0, 1]
$$

---

### Step 3: Analyze the Logarithmic Function

Now consider the function:
$$
y = \frac{1}{2} \log_2 (\sin x)
$$

Since $\sin x \in (0, 1]$, the logarithm $\log_2 (\sin x)$ is defined and takes values in:
$$
(-\infty, 0]
$$

Multiplying this interval by $ \frac{1}{2} $, the range of $ y $ becomes:
$$
(-\infty, 0]
$$

---

### Step 4: Confirm the Behavior at the Endpoints

- As $ x \to 0^\circ $ or $ x \to 180^\circ $, $\sin x \to 0^+$, so $\log_2 (\sqrt{\sin x}) \to -\infty$.
- At $ x = 90^\circ $, $\sin x = 1$, so $\log_2 (\sqrt{1}) = \log_2 (1) = 0$.

Therefore, the function $ y $ can take **any real value less than or equal to 0**, and it is **continuous** over the interval $ (0^\circ, 180^\circ) $, which confirms that the range is all real numbers from $ -\infty $ to 0, inclusive.

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{(-\infty, 0]}
$$
