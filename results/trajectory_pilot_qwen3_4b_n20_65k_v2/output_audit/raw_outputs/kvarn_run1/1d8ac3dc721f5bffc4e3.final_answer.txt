

To solve the problem, we need to determine the number of students, $ N $, who attended the archery class this week, given the following conditions:

1. **The number of students is between 30 and 50.**
2. **Normally, the students can be divided into groups of equal size.** This implies that $ N $ is **not prime**, because a prime number cannot be divided into equal groups of more than one (except for 1 and itself).
3. **This week, Betty noticed that she could not break the students into groups of equal size.** This means that $ N $ is **prime**.
4. **When Betty and Wilma joined the students, the total number of archers was still not divisible into equal groups.** So, $ N + 2 $ is also **prime**.

---

### Step-by-step Analysis:

- The number of students, $ N $, is **between 30 and 50**.
- Since the students **usually** form groups of equal size, $ N $ is **not prime**.
- But this week, **Betty could not** form equal groups, so $ N $ is **prime**.
- Then, **Wilma noticed** that even after adding 2 (Betty and Wilma), the total number $ N + 2 $ is still **not divisible into equal groups**, so $ N + 2 $ is also **prime**.

This means that $ N $ is a **prime number** such that $ N + 2 $ is also **prime**. These are known as **twin primes**.

---

### Step 1: List all prime numbers between 30 and 50

The prime numbers in this range are:

- 31
- 37
- 41
- 43
- 47

---

### Step 2: Check which of these primes have $ N + 2 $ also prime

- $ 31 + 2 = 33 $ → Not prime
- $ 37 + 2 = 39 $ → Not prime
- $ 41 + 2 = 43 $ → **Prime**
- $ 43 + 2 = 45 $ → Not prime
- $ 47 + 2 = 49 $ → Not prime

Only **41** and **43** are twin primes in this range. Since $ N + 2 = 43 $ is also prime, and $ N = 41 $ is between 30 and 50, this fits all the conditions.

---

### Final Conclusion:

The only number between 30 and 50 that is a prime and such that $ N + 2 $ is also prime is **41**.

Thus, the number of students who showed up to the archery class this week is:

$$
\boxed{41}
$$
