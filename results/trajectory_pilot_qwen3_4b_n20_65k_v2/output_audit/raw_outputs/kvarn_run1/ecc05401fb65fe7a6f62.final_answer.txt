

To determine the **range** of the function  
$$
f(x) = (\arccos x)^2 + (\arcsin x)^2,
$$  
we begin by leveraging a key identity from trigonometry:

$$
\arccos x + \arcsin x = \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad \text{for all } x \in [-1, 1].
$$

This identity allows us to express $ f(x) $ in terms of a single inverse trigonometric function. Let us define:

$$
y = \arcsin x \Rightarrow \arccos x = \frac{\pi}{2} - y.
$$

Substituting into $ f(x) $, we get:

$$
f(x) = \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - y \right)^2 + y^2.
$$

Expanding this expression:

$$
f(x) = \left( \frac{\pi^2}{4} - \pi y + y^2 \right) + y^2 = 2y^2 - \pi y + \frac{\pi^2}{4}.
$$

Now, since $ y = \arcsin x $, and the domain of $ \arcsin x $ is $ [-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}] $, we are looking to find the **range** of the quadratic function:

$$
g(y) = 2y^2 - \pi y + \frac{\pi^2}{4}, \quad \text{for } y \in \left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right].
$$

---

### Step 1: Analyze the Quadratic Function

This is a **quadratic function** in $ y $, with:

- Leading coefficient $ a = 2 > 0 $, so the parabola opens **upwards**.
- Vertex at $ y = \frac{-b}{2a} = \frac{\pi}{4} $, which lies within the interval $ [-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}] $.

Since the parabola opens upwards, the **minimum** value occurs at the vertex, and the **maximum** occurs at one of the endpoints of the interval.

---

### Step 2: Evaluate the Function at Key Points

- **At the vertex $ y = \frac{\pi}{4} $:**

$$
g\left( \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = 2\left( \frac{\pi}{4} \right)^2 - \pi \left( \frac{\pi}{4} \right) + \frac{\pi^2}{4}
= \frac{\pi^2}{8} - \frac{\pi^2}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{4} = \frac{\pi^2}{8}.
$$

- **At $ y = -\frac{\pi}{2} $:**

$$
g\left( -\frac{\pi}{2} \right) = 2\left( \frac{\pi^2}{4} \right) - \pi \left( -\frac{\pi}{2} \right) + \frac{\pi^2}{4}
= \frac{\pi^2}{2} + \frac{\pi^2}{2} + \frac{\pi^2}{4} = \frac{5\pi^2}{4}.
$$

- **At $ y = \frac{\pi}{2} $:**

$$
g\left( \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = 2\left( \frac{\pi^2}{4} \right) - \pi \left( \frac{\pi}{2} \right) + \frac{\pi^2}{4}
= \frac{\pi^2}{2} - \frac{\pi^2}{2} + \frac{\pi^2}{4} = \frac{\pi^2}{4}.
$$

---

### Step 3: Conclude the Range

Since the function is continuous on the interval $ [-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}] $, and the minimum occurs at $ y = \frac{\pi}{4} $, while the maximum occurs at $ y = -\frac{\pi}{2} $, the **range** of $ f(x) $ is:

$$
\left[ \frac{\pi^2}{8}, \frac{5\pi^2}{4} \right].
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{\left[ \dfrac{\pi^2}{8}, \dfrac{5\pi^2}{4} \right]}
