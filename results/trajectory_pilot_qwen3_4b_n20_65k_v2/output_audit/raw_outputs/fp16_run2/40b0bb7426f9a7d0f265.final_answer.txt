

To find the maximum value of the expression  
$$
|1 + z| + |1 - z + z^2|
$$  
given that $ |z| = 1 $, we begin by recognizing that any complex number $ z $ on the unit circle can be written in the form  
$$
z = e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta
$$  
for some real number $ \theta \in [0, 2\pi) $.

---

### Step 1: Simplify the First Term

We compute:
$$
|1 + z| = |1 + e^{i\theta}| = |e^{i\theta/2}(e^{-i\theta/2} + e^{i\theta/2})| = |2e^{i\theta/2} \cos(\theta/2)| = 2|\cos(\theta/2)|
$$

---

### Step 2: Simplify the Second Term

We compute:
$$
|1 - z + z^2| = |1 - e^{i\theta} + e^{i2\theta}|
$$
Let’s compute the modulus squared:
$$
|1 - e^{i\theta} + e^{i2\theta}|^2 = (1 - e^{i\theta} + e^{i2\theta})(1 - e^{-i\theta} + e^{-i2\theta})
$$
Expanding this and simplifying using trigonometric identities, we find:
$$
|1 - z + z^2|^2 = 4\cos^2\theta - 4\cos\theta + 1 = (2\cos\theta - 1)^2
$$
Thus:
$$
|1 - z + z^2| = |2\cos\theta - 1|
$$

---

### Step 3: Combine the Two Terms

Now the original expression becomes:
$$
|1 + z| + |1 - z + z^2| = 2|\cos(\theta/2)| + |2\cos\theta - 1|
$$

Let’s define:
$$
f(\theta) = 2|\cos(\theta/2)| + |2\cos\theta - 1|
$$

We aim to find the **maximum** of this function over $ \theta \in [0, 2\pi) $.

---

### Step 4: Analyze the Function

We analyze the function by splitting the domain into intervals based on the sign of $ \cos(\theta/2) $ and $ 2\cos\theta - 1 $, which are both piecewise functions due to the absolute values.

Let’s define $ x = \cos(\theta/2) $, then $ \cos\theta = 2x^2 - 1 $, and the function becomes:
$$
f(x) = 2|x| + |2(2x^2 - 1) - 1| = 2|x| + |4x^2 - 3|
$$

We now consider the function:
$$
f(x) = 2|x| + |4x^2 - 3|
$$
over the domain of $ x = \cos(\theta/2) $, which is $ x \in [-1, 1] $.

---

### Step 5: Maximize the Function

We analyze the function in different intervals based on the sign of $ x $ and $ 4x^2 - 3 $:

- **Case 1:** $ x \in [0, \sqrt{3}/2] $, then $ 4x^2 - 3 \leq 0 $, so:
  $$
  f(x) = 2x - (4x^2 - 3) = -4x^2 + 2x + 3
  $$
  This is a downward-opening parabola with maximum at vertex $ x = \frac{1}{4} $, giving:
  $$
  f\left(\frac{1}{4}\right) = -4\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^2 + 2\left(\frac{1}{4}\right) + 3 = -\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2} + 3 = \frac{13}{4}
  $$

- **Case 2:** $ x \in [-1, -\sqrt{3}/2] $, then $ 4x^2 - 3 \geq 0 $, so:
  $$
  f(x) = -2x + (4x^2 - 3) = 4x^2 - 2x - 3
  $$
  This is an upward-opening parabola, and the maximum occurs at the **left endpoint** $ x = -1 $, giving:
  $$
  f(-1) = 4(1) - 2(-1) - 3 = 4 + 2 - 3 = 3
  $$

- **Other intervals** (e.g., $ x \in [\sqrt{3}/2, 1] $, $ x \in [-\sqrt{3}/2, 0] $) yield values less than or equal to $ \frac{13}{4} $, as shown in the detailed analysis.

---

### Step 6: Conclusion

The maximum value of the expression occurs at $ x = \frac{1}{4} $ or $ x = -\frac{1}{4} $, corresponding to specific values of $ \theta $, and the maximum value is:

$$
\boxed{\dfrac{13}{4}}
$$
