

To solve the problem, we are given that the polynomial  
$$
f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x - 1
$$  
is a factor of  
$$
g(x) = x^9 + px^6 + qx^3 + r.
$$  
This means that $ f(x) $ divides $ g(x) $, and thus, the roots of $ f(x) $ are also roots of $ g(x) $. Therefore, for any root $ \alpha $ of $ f(x) $, we must have  
$$
g(\alpha) = \alpha^9 + p\alpha^6 + q\alpha^3 + r = 0.
$$

---

### Step 1: Use the fact that $ \alpha^3 = 3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1 $

Since $ \alpha $ is a root of $ f(x) $, we know that  
$$
\alpha^3 = 3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1.
$$  
We can use this to express higher powers of $ \alpha $ in terms of lower-degree terms.

---

### Step 2: Compute $ \alpha^6 $ and $ \alpha^9 $ in terms of $ \alpha^2, \alpha, $ and constants

We compute:

- $ \alpha^4 = \alpha \cdot \alpha^3 = \alpha(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) = 3\alpha^3 - 4\alpha^2 + \alpha = 3(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) - 4\alpha^2 + \alpha = 5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 3 $

- $ \alpha^6 = (\alpha^3)^2 = (3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1)^2 = 9\alpha^4 - 24\alpha^3 + 22\alpha^2 - 8\alpha + 1 $

Substitute $ \alpha^4 = 5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 3 $ and $ \alpha^3 = 3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1 $ into the expression for $ \alpha^6 $:

$$
\alpha^6 = 9(5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 3) - 24(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) + 22\alpha^2 - 8\alpha + 1
$$
$$
= 45\alpha^2 - 99\alpha + 27 - 72\alpha^2 + 96\alpha - 24 + 22\alpha^2 - 8\alpha + 1
$$
$$
= (45 - 72 + 22)\alpha^2 + (-99 + 96 - 8)\alpha + (27 - 24 + 1)
$$
$$
= -5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 4
$$

Now compute $ \alpha^9 = \alpha^3 \cdot \alpha^6 $:

$$
\alpha^9 = (3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1)(-5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 4)
$$

Expanding this:

$$
= -15\alpha^4 - 33\alpha^3 + 12\alpha^2 + 20\alpha^3 + 44\alpha^2 - 16\alpha - 5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 4
$$
$$
= -15\alpha^4 - 13\alpha^3 + (12 + 44 - 5)\alpha^2 + (-16 - 11)\alpha + 4
$$
$$
= -15\alpha^4 - 13\alpha^3 + 51\alpha^2 - 27\alpha + 4
$$

Now substitute $ \alpha^4 = 5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 3 $ and $ \alpha^3 = 3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1 $:

$$
\alpha^9 = -15(5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 3) - 13(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) + 51\alpha^2 - 27\alpha + 4
$$
$$
= -75\alpha^2 + 165\alpha - 45 - 39\alpha^2 + 52\alpha - 13 + 51\alpha^2 - 27\alpha + 4
$$
$$
= (-75 - 39 + 51)\alpha^2 + (165 + 52 - 27)\alpha + (-45 - 13 + 4)
$$
$$
= -63\alpha^2 + 190\alpha - 54
$$

---

### Step 3: Plug into $ g(\alpha) = 0 $

We now substitute into the expression for $ g(\alpha) $:

$$
g(\alpha) = \alpha^9 + p\alpha^6 + q\alpha^3 + r = (-63\alpha^2 + 190\alpha - 54) + p(-5\alpha^2 - 11\alpha + 4) + q(3\alpha^2 - 4\alpha + 1) + r
$$

Group like terms:

- Coefficient of $ \alpha^2 $: $ -63 - 5p + 3q $
- Coefficient of $ \alpha $: $ 190 - 11p - 4q $
- Constant term: $ -54 + 4p + q + r $

Set each coefficient to zero:

1. $ -63 - 5p + 3q = 0 $  
2. $ 190 - 11p - 4q = 0 $  
3. $ -54 + 4p + q + r = 0 $

---

### Step 4: Solve the system of equations

From equation (1):  
$$
3q = 5p + 63 \Rightarrow q = \frac{5p + 63}{3}
$$

Substitute into equation (2):  
$$
190 - 11p - 4\left(\frac{5p + 63}{3}\right) = 0
$$
$$
190 - 11p - \frac{20p + 252}{3} = 0
$$
Multiply through by 3:  
$$
570 - 33p - 20p - 252 = 0 \Rightarrow 318 - 53p = 0 \Rightarrow p = 6
$$

Now find $ q $:  
$$
q = \frac{5(6) + 63}{3} = \frac{30 + 63}{3} = \frac{93}{3} = 31
$$

Now find $ r $ from equation (3):  
$$
-54 + 4(6) + 31 + r = 0 \Rightarrow -54 + 24 + 31 + r = 0 \Rightarrow 1 + r = 0 \Rightarrow r = -1
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{(6, 31, -1)}
$$
