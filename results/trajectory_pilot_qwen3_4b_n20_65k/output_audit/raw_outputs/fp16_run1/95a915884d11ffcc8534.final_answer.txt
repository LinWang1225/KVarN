

To solve the problem, we are given two key pieces of information about a two-digit number:

1. The **sum of its digits** is 13.
2. The **difference between the number and its reversed version** is 27.

We are asked to find the **sum of the original number and its reversed version**.

---

### Step 1: Represent the Number Algebraically

Let the original two-digit number be represented as:

$$
N = 10a + b
$$

Where:
- $ a $ is the tens digit (must be between 1 and 9, since it's a two-digit number),
- $ b $ is the units digit (must be between 0 and 9).

The reversed number is:

$$
R = 10b + a
$$

---

### Step 2: Use the Given Conditions

**Condition 1:** The sum of the digits is 13.

$$
a + b = 13 \tag{1}
$$

**Condition 2:** The difference between the number and its reverse is 27.

$$
(10a + b) - (10b + a) = 27
$$

Simplify the equation:

$$
10a + b - 10b - a = 27 \\
9a - 9b = 27 \\
a - b = 3 \tag{2}
$$

---

### Step 3: Solve the System of Equations

We now have two equations:

- $ a + b = 13 $ (Equation 1)
- $ a - b = 3 $ (Equation 2)

Add the two equations:

$$
(a + b) + (a - b) = 13 + 3 \\
2a = 16 \\
a = 8
$$

Substitute $ a = 8 $ into Equation 1:

$$
8 + b = 13 \\
b = 5
$$

So the original number is:

$$
N = 10a + b = 10 \cdot 8 + 5 = 85
$$

The reversed number is:

$$
R = 10b + a = 10 \cdot 5 + 8 = 58
$$

---

### Step 4: Find the Sum of the Original and Reversed Numbers

$$
N + R = 85 + 58 = 143
$$

Alternatively, we can use a general formula for the sum of a two-digit number and its reverse:

$$
N + R = (10a + b) + (10b + a) = 11a + 11b = 11(a + b)
$$

Since $ a + b = 13 $, we get:

$$
N + R = 11 \cdot 13 = 143
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{143}
$$
