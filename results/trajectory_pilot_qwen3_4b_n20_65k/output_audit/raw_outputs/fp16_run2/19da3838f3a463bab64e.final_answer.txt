

To find the value of the expression $ x^3 - 2x + 1 $, we are given the quadratic equation:

$$
x^2 - x - 1 = 0
$$

---

### Step 1: Express $ x^2 $ in terms of $ x $

From the given equation, we can rearrange it to:

$$
x^2 = x + 1
$$

This is a key identity that allows us to reduce higher powers of $ x $ in terms of lower powers.

---

### Step 2: Compute $ x^3 $

We multiply both sides of the equation $ x^2 = x + 1 $ by $ x $:

$$
x^3 = x \cdot x^2 = x(x + 1) = x^2 + x
$$

Now substitute $ x^2 = x + 1 $ into the expression:

$$
x^3 = (x + 1) + x = 2x + 1
$$

---

### Step 3: Substitute into the original expression

We now substitute $ x^3 = 2x + 1 $ into the expression $ x^3 - 2x + 1 $:

$$
x^3 - 2x + 1 = (2x + 1) - 2x + 1
$$

Simplify the expression:

$$
(2x + 1) - 2x + 1 = 2x - 2x + 1 + 1 = 2
$$

---

### Step 4: Verification via polynomial division

We can also verify this by performing polynomial division of $ x^3 - 2x + 1 $ by $ x^2 - x - 1 $:

- Divide $ x^3 - 2x + 1 $ by $ x^2 - x - 1 $, the quotient is $ x $, and the remainder is:

$$
(x^3 - 2x + 1) - x(x^2 - x - 1) = x^3 - 2x + 1 - (x^3 - x^2 - x) = x^2 - x + 1
$$

Now, since $ x^2 = x + 1 $, we substitute:

$$
x^2 - x + 1 = (x + 1) - x + 1 = 2
$$

Thus, the remainder is 2, and since the divisor is zero, the value of the expression is:

$$
x^3 - 2x + 1 = 2
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{2}
$$
