

To solve the problem, we need to determine the number of students who attended the archery class this week, given the following conditions:

1. The number of students is **between 30 and 50**.
2. **Normally**, the students can be divided into **groups of equal size**.
3. **This week**, Betty noticed that she **could not** divide the students into groups of equal size.
4. **Then**, when **Betty and Wilma** joined the students, the total number **still could not** be divided into groups of equal size.

---

### Step 1: Understanding the Implication of the First Condition

The key phrase is: *"Usually the students break up into groups of equal size."* This implies that the number of students is **not prime**—since a prime number cannot be divided into equal groups of size greater than 1 (except 1 and itself). Therefore, **this week's number of students must be a prime number**, because that is the only case where the students **cannot** be divided into equal groups.

So, the number of students this week is a **prime number between 30 and 50**.

---

### Step 2: List of Primes Between 30 and 50

The prime numbers in that range are:

- 31
- 37
- 41
- 43
- 47

---

### Step 3: Apply the Second Condition

When **Betty and Wilma** join the students, the total number of archers becomes:

$$
\text{Original number of students} + 2
$$

This new total **still cannot** be divided into equal groups, meaning it must also be **prime**.

So, we are looking for a **prime number between 30 and 50** such that **adding 2 to it results in another prime number**.

Let’s test each candidate:

- **31 + 2 = 33** → Not prime (3 × 11)
- **37 + 2 = 39** → Not prime (3 × 13)
- **41 + 2 = 43** → **Prime**
- **43 + 2 = 45** → Not prime (5 × 9)
- **47 + 2 = 49** → Not prime (7 × 7)

Only **41** satisfies the condition that both the original number and the number after adding 2 are **prime**.

---

### Step 4: Final Verification

- 41 is a prime number (between 30 and 50).
- 41 + 2 = 43, which is also a prime number.
- Therefore, both conditions are satisfied:
  - The students **could not** be divided into equal groups (41 is prime).
  - When Betty and Wilma join, the total is still **not divisible** into equal groups (43 is prime).

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{41}
$$
