

To find the value of the expression $ x^3 - 2x + 1 $, given that $ x^2 - x - 1 = 0 $, we can use the original equation to reduce higher powers of $ x $ to lower-degree expressions.

---

### Step 1: Use the original equation to express $ x^2 $

From the given equation:

$$
x^2 - x - 1 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x^2 = x + 1
$$

This is a key identity that allows us to express higher powers of $ x $ in terms of $ x $ and constants.

---

### Step 2: Express $ x^3 $ in terms of $ x $

Multiply both sides of the equation $ x^2 = x + 1 $ by $ x $:

$$
x^3 = x \cdot x^2 = x(x + 1) = x^2 + x
$$

Now substitute $ x^2 = x + 1 $ into the expression:

$$
x^3 = (x + 1) + x = 2x + 1
$$

---

### Step 3: Substitute into the target expression

We are asked to evaluate:

$$
x^3 - 2x + 1
$$

Substitute $ x^3 = 2x + 1 $ into the expression:

$$
x^3 - 2x + 1 = (2x + 1) - 2x + 1 = 2x + 1 - 2x + 1 = 2
$$

---

### Step 4: Verification via polynomial division

We can also verify this by performing polynomial division of $ x^3 - 2x + 1 $ by $ x^2 - x - 1 $, which is the original equation.

Divide $ x^3 - 2x + 1 $ by $ x^2 - x - 1 $:

- First term: $ x^3 \div x^2 = x $
- Multiply $ x $ by $ x^2 - x - 1 $: $ x^3 - x^2 - x $
- Subtract from the original polynomial:  
  $$
  (x^3 - 2x + 1) - (x^3 - x^2 - x) = x^2 - x + 1
  $$
- Now divide $ x^2 - x + 1 $ by $ x^2 - x - 1 $:  
  $$
  (x^2 - x + 1) - (x^2 - x - 1) = 2
  $$

So the remainder is 2, and since $ x^2 - x - 1 = 0 $, the value of the expression is:

$$
x^3 - 2x + 1 = 2
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{2}
$$
