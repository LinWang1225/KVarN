

We are given the expression:

$$
a(2a + b) - 2a^2 + ab
$$

where $ a $ and $ b $ are **different prime numbers greater than 2**. Our goal is to determine how many **whole-number divisors** this expression has.

---

### Step 1: Simplify the Expression

Start by expanding the first term:

$$
a(2a + b) = 2a^2 + ab
$$

Now substitute back into the original expression:

$$
2a^2 + ab - 2a^2 + ab
$$

Simplify by combining like terms:

$$
(2a^2 - 2a^2) + (ab + ab) = 0 + 2ab = 2ab
$$

So the expression simplifies to:

$$
2ab
$$

---

### Step 2: Analyze the Structure of the Expression

We now know that the expression simplifies to $ 2ab $, where:

- $ a $ and $ b $ are **distinct primes greater than 2**, so they are **odd** and **not equal to 2**.
- $ 2 $ is a **prime number**, and since $ a $ and $ b $ are not 2, the prime factorization of $ 2ab $ is:

$$
2^1 \times a^1 \times b^1
$$

This is the **prime factorization** of the number $ 2ab $, with all exponents being 1.

---

### Step 3: Use the Divisor Count Formula

The number of **positive whole-number divisors** of a number is determined by the formula:

$$
\text{Number of divisors} = (e_1 + 1)(e_2 + 1)\cdots(e_n + 1)
$$

where $ e_1, e_2, \dots, e_n $ are the exponents in the prime factorization.

In this case, the prime factorization is:

$$
2^1 \times a^1 \times b^1
$$

So the number of divisors is:

$$
(1 + 1)(1 + 1)(1 + 1) = 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8
$$

---

### Step 4: Confirm with Examples

Let’s test with specific values of $ a $ and $ b $:

- If $ a = 3 $, $ b = 5 $, then $ 2ab = 2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30 $
  - Divisors of 30: $ 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30 $ → 8 divisors
- If $ a = 5 $, $ b = 7 $, then $ 2ab = 2 \times 5 \times 7 = 70 $
  - Divisors of 70: $ 1, 2, 5, 7, 10, 14, 35, 70 $ → 8 divisors

In both cases, the number of divisors is 8, confirming the general result.

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{8}
$$
