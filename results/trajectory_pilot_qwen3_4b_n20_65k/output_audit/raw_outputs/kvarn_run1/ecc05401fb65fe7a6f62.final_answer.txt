

To determine the range of the function  
$$
f(x) = (\arccos x)^2 + (\arcsin x)^2,
$$  
we begin by leveraging a key identity from inverse trigonometric functions:

$$
\arccos x + \arcsin x = \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad \text{for all } x \in [-1, 1].
$$

Let us define $ \theta = \arccos x $. Then, by the identity,  
$$
\arcsin x = \frac{\pi}{2} - \theta.
$$

Substituting into the function, we get:
$$
f(x) = \theta^2 + \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - \theta \right)^2.
$$

Expanding the square:
$$
f(x) = \theta^2 + \left( \frac{\pi^2}{4} - \pi\theta + \theta^2 \right) = 2\theta^2 - \pi\theta + \frac{\pi^2}{4}.
$$

This is a quadratic function in $ \theta $, and since $ \theta = \arccos x $, the domain of $ \theta $ is $ [0, \pi] $, corresponding to $ x \in [-1, 1] $.

---

### Step 1: Analyze the Quadratic Function

Let:
$$
g(\theta) = 2\theta^2 - \pi\theta + \frac{\pi^2}{4}.
$$

This is a parabola that opens upwards (since the coefficient of $ \theta^2 $ is positive). The vertex of a parabola $ a\theta^2 + b\theta + c $ occurs at:
$$
\theta = -\frac{b}{2a} = \frac{\pi}{4}.
$$

So, the minimum value of $ g(\theta) $ occurs at $ \theta = \frac{\pi}{4} $, and the maximum occurs at one of the endpoints of the interval $ [0, \pi] $.

---

### Step 2: Evaluate at Critical Points

- At $ \theta = \frac{\pi}{4} $:
  $$
  g\left( \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = 2\left( \frac{\pi}{4} \right)^2 - \pi\left( \frac{\pi}{4} \right) + \frac{\pi^2}{4} = \frac{\pi^2}{8} - \frac{\pi^2}{4} + \frac{\pi^2}{4} = \frac{\pi^2}{8}.
  $$

- At $ \theta = 0 $:
  $$
  g(0) = 2(0)^2 - \pi(0) + \frac{\pi^2}{4} = \frac{\pi^2}{4}.
  $$

- At $ \theta = \pi $:
  $$
  g(\pi) = 2\pi^2 - \pi^2 + \frac{\pi^2}{4} = \pi^2 + \frac{\pi^2}{4} = \frac{5\pi^2}{4}.
  $$

Thus, the minimum value of $ f(x) $ is $ \frac{\pi^2}{8} $, and the maximum is $ \frac{5\pi^2}{4} $.

---

### Step 3: Confirm the Behavior of $ f(x) $

We can also confirm this by analyzing the derivative of $ f(x) $:
$$
f'(x) = \frac{d}{dx} \left[ (\arccos x)^2 + (\arcsin x)^2 \right] = \frac{2\pi - 4\arccos x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}.
$$

Setting the numerator to zero gives:
$$
2\pi - 4\arccos x = 0 \Rightarrow \arccos x = \frac{\pi}{4} \Rightarrow x = \cos\left( \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.
$$

This confirms that the function has a critical point at $ x = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} $, and the function is decreasing on $ [-1, \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}] $ and increasing on $ [\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, 1] $, with the minimum at $ x = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} $ and the maximum at $ x = -1 $.

---

### Final Conclusion

The function $ f(x) = (\arccos x)^2 + (\arcsin x)^2 $ is defined on $ x \in [-1, 1] $, and its range is determined by the values of the quadratic function $ g(\theta) = 2\theta^2 - \pi\theta + \frac{\pi^2}{4} $ over $ \theta \in [0, \pi] $.

The minimum value is $ \frac{\pi^2}{8} $, and the maximum is $ \frac{5\pi^2}{4} $.
