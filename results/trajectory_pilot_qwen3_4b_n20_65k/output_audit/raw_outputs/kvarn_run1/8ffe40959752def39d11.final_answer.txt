

To solve the equation:

$$
\sin \left( \tan^{-1} (x) + \cot^{-1} \left( \frac{1}{x} \right) \right) = \frac{1}{3}
$$

we begin by analyzing the relationship between the inverse trigonometric functions involved.

---

### Step 1: Use the identity for inverse cotangent

We recall the identity:

$$
\cot^{-1}(y) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{y}\right) \quad \text{for } y > 0
$$

and

$$
\cot^{-1}(y) = \pi + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{y}\right) \quad \text{for } y < 0
$$

In our case, the argument of the inverse cotangent is $\frac{1}{x}$, so we consider two cases based on the sign of $x$:

---

### Case 1: $x > 0$

Then:

$$
\cot^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = \tan^{-1}(x)
$$

So the expression becomes:

$$
\sin\left( \tan^{-1}(x) + \tan^{-1}(x) \right) = \sin(2\tan^{-1}(x)) = \frac{1}{3}
$$

We use the identity:

$$
\sin(2\theta) = \frac{2\tan\theta}{1 + \tan^2\theta}
$$

Let $\theta = \tan^{-1}(x)$, so $\tan\theta = x$, and:

$$
\sin(2\theta) = \frac{2x}{1 + x^2}
$$

Thus, the equation becomes:

$$
\frac{2x}{1 + x^2} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

Multiply both sides by $1 + x^2$:

$$
2x = \frac{1 + x^2}{3}
\Rightarrow 6x = 1 + x^2
\Rightarrow x^2 - 6x + 1 = 0
$$

Solving this quadratic:

$$
x = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{36 - 4}}{2} = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{32}}{2} = \frac{6 \pm 4\sqrt{2}}{2} = 3 \pm 2\sqrt{2}
$$

Since $x > 0$, both solutions are valid.

---

### Case 2: $x < 0$

Then:

$$
\cot^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = \pi + \tan^{-1}(x)
$$

So the expression becomes:

$$
\sin\left( \tan^{-1}(x) + \pi + \tan^{-1}(x) \right) = \sin(\pi + 2\tan^{-1}(x)) = \frac{1}{3}
$$

Using the identity:

$$
\sin(\pi + \alpha) = -\sin(\alpha)
$$

we get:

$$
-\sin(2\tan^{-1}(x)) = \frac{1}{3}
\Rightarrow \sin(2\tan^{-1}(x)) = -\frac{1}{3}
$$

Again, using the identity:

$$
\sin(2\theta) = \frac{2x}{1 + x^2}
$$

we have:

$$
\frac{2x}{1 + x^2} = -\frac{1}{3}
$$

Multiply both sides by $1 + x^2$:

$$
2x = -\frac{1 + x^2}{3}
\Rightarrow 6x = -1 - x^2
\Rightarrow x^2 + 6x + 1 = 0
$$

Solving this quadratic:

$$
x = \frac{-6 \pm \sqrt{36 - 4}}{2} = \frac{-6 \pm \sqrt{32}}{2} = \frac{-6 \pm 4\sqrt{2}}{2} = -3 \pm 2\sqrt{2}
$$

Since $x < 0$, both solutions are valid.

---

### Final Step: Combine All Solutions

From the two cases, we have the following four solutions:

$$
x = 3 + 2\sqrt{2}, \quad x = 3 - 2\sqrt{2}, \quad x = -3 + 2\sqrt{2}, \quad x = -3 - 2\sqrt{2}
$$

All of these are distinct and valid, as they are all real numbers and none is zero (which would make the original expression undefined).

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{-3 - 2\sqrt{2}}, \quad \boxed{-3 + 2\sqrt{2}}, \quad \boxed{3 - 2\sqrt{2}}, \quad \boxed{3 + 2\sqrt{2}}
$$
