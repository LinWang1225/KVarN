

To simplify the expression  
$$
\frac{4 + 6a}{5} - \frac{1 + 3a}{4}
$$  
into a single fraction, we need to find a common denominator and combine the two fractions.

---

### Step 1: Find the Least Common Denominator (LCD)

The denominators are 5 and 4. Since they are relatively prime, the least common denominator is:

$$
\text{LCD} = 5 \times 4 = 20
$$

---

### Step 2: Rewrite Each Fraction with the LCD

We rewrite each fraction so that the denominator is 20:

- For $\frac{4 + 6a}{5}$, multiply numerator and denominator by 4:
  $$
  \frac{4 + 6a}{5} = \frac{4(4 + 6a)}{20} = \frac{16 + 24a}{20}
  $$

- For $\frac{1 + 3a}{4}$, multiply numerator and denominator by 5:
  $$
  \frac{1 + 3a}{4} = \frac{5(1 + 3a)}{20} = \frac{5 + 15a}{20}
  $$

---

### Step 3: Subtract the Fractions

Now that both fractions have the same denominator, we can subtract the numerators:

$$
\frac{16 + 24a}{20} - \frac{5 + 15a}{20} = \frac{(16 + 24a) - (5 + 15a)}{20}
$$

Simplify the numerator:

$$
16 - 5 = 11,\quad 24a - 15a = 9a
$$

So the numerator becomes:

$$
11 + 9a
$$

---

### Step 4: Write the Final Answer

The expression as a single fraction is:

$$
\frac{11 + 9a}{20}
$$

To follow standard form, we can write the terms in descending order of degree:

$$
\frac{9a + 11}{20}
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{\dfrac{9a + 11}{20}}
$$
