

To solve the equation:

$$
10^x - 10 = 9990
$$

we begin by isolating the exponential term. Add 10 to both sides:

$$
10^x = 9990 + 10 = 10000
$$

Now, we have:

$$
10^x = 10000
$$

We recognize that $10000$ is a power of 10. Specifically:

$$
10000 = 10^4
$$

Therefore, the equation becomes:

$$
10^x = 10^4
$$

Since the bases are the same, the exponents must be equal:

$$
x = 4
$$

---

To verify, substitute $x = 4$ back into the original equation:

$$
10^4 - 10 = 10000 - 10 = 9990
$$

This matches the right-hand side of the equation, confirming the solution is correct.

---

Alternatively, we can solve using logarithms. Taking the base-10 logarithm of both sides of $10^x = 10000$:

$$
\log_{10}(10^x) = \log_{10}(10000)
$$

$$
x = \log_{10}(10000)
$$

Since $10000 = 10^4$, we have:

$$
x = \log_{10}(10^4) = 4
$$

---

Another method: express $10000$ as $10^4$, and since the exponential function $10^x$ is strictly increasing and one-to-one, there is only one solution to the equation $10^x = 10000$, which is $x = 4$.

---

Thus, the value of $x$ is:

$$
\boxed{4}
$$
