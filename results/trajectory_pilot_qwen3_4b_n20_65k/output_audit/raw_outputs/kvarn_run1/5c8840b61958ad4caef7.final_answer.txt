

To find the **greatest possible value** of the expression  
$$
\left(a + \frac{1}{b}\right)\left(\frac{1}{b} - a\right)
$$  
given the constraints  
$$
-6 \leq a \leq -2 \quad \text{and} \quad 3 \leq b \leq 5,
$$  
we begin by simplifying the expression.

---

### Step 1: Simplify the Expression

We recognize that the expression is of the form:
$$
(x + y)(x - y) = x^2 - y^2
$$
Letting $ x = \frac{1}{b} $ and $ y = a $, we get:
$$
\left(a + \frac{1}{b}\right)\left(\frac{1}{b} - a\right) = \left(\frac{1}{b}\right)^2 - a^2 = \frac{1}{b^2} - a^2
$$

So the expression simplifies to:
$$
\frac{1}{b^2} - a^2
$$

---

### Step 2: Analyze the Expression

We are to **maximize** the expression:
$$
\frac{1}{b^2} - a^2
$$
under the constraints:
- $ a \in [-6, -2] $
- $ b \in [3, 5] $

Note that:
- $ \frac{1}{b^2} $ is a **decreasing function** of $ b $, so it is **maximized** when $ b $ is **smallest** (i.e., $ b = 3 $).
- $ a^2 $ is a **positive** function, and it is **minimized** when $ a $ is **closest to zero** (i.e., $ a = -2 $).

Thus, to **maximize** $ \frac{1}{b^2} - a^2 $, we should:
- Maximize $ \frac{1}{b^2} $: choose $ b = 3 $
- Minimize $ a^2 $: choose $ a = -2 $

---

### Step 3: Compute the Maximum Value

Substitute $ a = -2 $ and $ b = 3 $ into the simplified expression:
$$
\frac{1}{b^2} - a^2 = \frac{1}{3^2} - (-2)^2 = \frac{1}{9} - 4 = \frac{1 - 36}{9} = -\frac{35}{9}
$$

---

### Step 4: Confirm It's the Greatest Possible Value

We now verify that this is indeed the **greatest possible value** of the expression under the given constraints.

- For any other value of $ a $ in $ [-6, -2] $, $ a^2 $ increases, making $ \frac{1}{b^2} - a^2 $ **smaller**.
- For any other value of $ b $ in $ [3, 5] $, $ \frac{1}{b^2} $ decreases, making the expression **smaller**.

Therefore, the **maximum** occurs at $ a = -2 $, $ b = 3 $, and the value is:
$$
\boxed{-\dfrac{35}{9}}
$$
